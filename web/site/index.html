How it works

  1. 1

    Scan the barcode

    Packs we've already checked open at once.

  2. 2

    New pack? One photo of the ingredients list

    The block that starts with "Ingredients:". Not the nutrition table or the licence number. The front of the pack is optional.

  3. 3

    Each person's answer first

    Allergens and your family's avoid lists come first. The rest waits under "Worth knowing", each line with its source.

How we check

Four things you can hold us to. How we check a label explains every word on a result.

AI reads. Rules decide.
An AI reads the words on the label. It doesn't judge anything. Fixed rules, based on FSSAI regulations, check each ingredient and your family's avoid list.
FSSAI is the basis
69 additives, each with its FSSAI Appendix A entry and the date we checked it. Rules in other countries are shown only as extra information.
Read from the real pack
Every pack in our catalogue was reviewed against photos of the real pack before it joined.
Unclear means "couldn't confirm"
When a photo is hard to read, the allergen line says it couldn't confirm, and the app asks for a clearer one.

It isn't medical advice: for a health condition, ask your doctor. Brands can't pay to change or hide what InsideLabel shows.

Why I built InsideLabel

I'm Sushil Behera, and I have a young son. When he visits the neighbours, someone always hands him a biscuit, a chocolate or a packet of chips. Kind people, kind gestures. But each time I turned the pack over, I couldn't tell what he was actually eating: the ingredient list was tiny, full of codes like INS 322 and 150d, and nothing on the pack explained them.

I've worked with data for years, so I built the tool I wanted: scan the pack and see what's in it, in plain words, starting with anything on your family's avoid list.

InsideLabel is paid for from my own money. No food company pays us, and none can change what we show. I read every reported mistake myself.
