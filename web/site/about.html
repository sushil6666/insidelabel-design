How a result is made

  1. An AI reads the label and returns the words printed on it. It never decides anything.
  2. A second, independent reader checks the photo for additive numbers the first one may have missed. It can only add a number printed on the pack, never remove one.
  3. Fixed rules check each ingredient against our tables. Every additive in the tables is checked against the FSSAI regulations and the Codex international list, with the date it was checked.
  4. When the photo is too hard to read, the app says so and asks for a clearer one, rather than guessing.
  5. A person reviews every new pack against its photos before it joins the catalogue. After that, scanning its barcode shows the reviewed result, with the date it was last checked.

Rules in other countries are shown only as extra information, when you ask for them. They never change a result. How we check a label explains each word you see on a result.

Where our facts come from

Every sentence the app says about an ingredient comes from a document we read ourselves. If no source says something, the app doesn't say it. What research found, the ingredient notes and the nutrition thresholds each link to their source on the result.

  • Permitted in India: FSSAI's Food Products Standards and Food Additives Regulations, 2011 (Appendix A), and its Labelling and Display Regulations, 2020.
  • Additive names and classes: the Codex Alimentarius list of additive names (CAC/GL 36).
  • Ingredient notes and the nutrition thresholds: the Indian Council of Medical Research's Dietary Guidelines for Indians (ICMR-NIN, 2024) and the World Health Organization.
  • What research found about an additive: the study itself, or the opinion of a regulator such as the European Food Safety Authority, the Joint FAO/WHO Expert Committee on Food Additives or the UK Food Standards Agency.

Each additive in our tables records where in the FSSAI rules it is allowed and the date we checked.
