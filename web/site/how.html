How we check a label

An AI reads the words printed on the pack. It never decides anything. Fixed rules then check each ingredient against our tables, which are checked against FSSAI's regulations. This page explains the words you see on a result.

No score

InsideLabel doesn't give a pack a score, a grade or stars. One number can't say what matters in your family: the same biscuit can be on one person's avoid list and not another's. So we show what the label says, person by person, with the source for each line, and you decide.

Your avoid list

The foods you, or a child you shop for, need to avoid, such as peanut or milk. We check every pack for them first and put the answer at the top of the result. We only report what the label prints: a pack can leave out traces, so we only ever say what is or isn't listed. When the photo is hard to read, we say we can't confirm, rather than guess.

Allergen line

The line on the pack that starts with "Contains" or "May contain". We read it together with the ingredients. If the photo doesn't show one, we say so and ask you to check the pack.

Sugar

Every added sugar on the list, under any of its names: sugar, liquid glucose, invert syrup, dextrose, candy sugar syrup and others. Ingredients are listed from most to least, so a sugar near the start is one of the main ingredients. When sugar appears under several names, each name sits lower on the list than their total would.

Industrial ingredients

Ingredients rarely used in home cooking, such as glucose syrup, maltodextrin, emulsifiers, modified starch or added flavouring. Researchers use them to spot "ultra-processed" food (the NOVA classification, group 4). It describes how a food is made. It is not advice about whether a food is right for you.

Additive

A substance added to do a job, such as a colour, a preservative or a raising agent. Most have an INS number, the international code printed on the pack, for example "Colour (129)". We check each one against FSSAI's list of additives permitted in India.

Permitted in India

FSSAI's additive rules (Appendix A of its food additive regulations) allow it in food, either freely, or up to a limit that depends on the kind of food. We record where in the rules it is allowed and the date we checked.

Worth knowing

An additive with research, or a regulator's opinion, that a shopper may want to read. It is permitted in India. Tap its tile to see what the research found, in plain words, and a link to the source. We write these only from the study or the regulator's own document.

Not permitted in India

An additive number that FSSAI's additive rules (Appendix A) give no use in any food. It is the most serious thing we say about a pack, so we show it only when the whole list was read clearly. We quote what the label prints and ask you to check the pack: if we misread the number, tap "Report a mistake". A pack with a barcode also goes to a person to check.

Ingredients worth knowing

A few ordinary ingredients have advice written about them, such as hydrogenated fat, palm oil or refined flour (maida). We show a short note from the Indian Council of Medical Research's Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024), the World Health Organization or FSSAI's own definitions, with a link to each source. A note is information only: it never changes what we say about your avoid list or an additive. Its tile means the headline says "A few things worth knowing" rather than "No concerns found in the ingredients".

Nutrition table

When the photo shows the nutrition table, we read it, then check every number twice: a second, independent reader has to find it in the same photo, and the table has to add up (a part can't be more than its whole, and the per serving column has to fit the per 100 g one). A number that fails either check isn't shown; we don't guess which one was right. We then set energy, added sugar and salt beside the Indian Council of Medical Research's thresholds for packaged food (Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2024), and a serving beside its 25 g of sugar and 5 g of salt a day for adults. Trans fat above zero gets its own tile. The table is information only, the same way: its tiles can change "No concerns found in the ingredients" to "A few things worth knowing". It adds a line only for a child under 2 in your household, when salt or added sugar is over that threshold.

Tiles

The short boxes under the headline are the things on this pack worth a look. Tap one to open its own page: what it is, what the source says, the words on the label, and the source.

Other

Everything else on the list, such as flour, oil, milk, salt or spices.

At a glance

The ring counts the ingredients of each kind. It counts ingredients, not weight: packs rarely print amounts. The strip shows the list in the order printed, so what sits on the left there is most of.
