    How we check a label
    An AI reads the words printed on the pack. It never decides anything. Fixed rules then check each
      ingredient against our tables, which are checked against FSSAI's regulations. This page explains the
      words you see on a result.
    Your avoid list
    The foods you, or a child you shop for, need to avoid, such as peanut or milk. We check every pack for
      them first and put the answer at the top of the result. We only report what the label prints: a pack
      can leave out traces, so we only ever say what is or isn't listed. When the photo is hard to read, we
      say we can't confirm, rather than guess.
    Allergen line
    The line on the pack that starts with "Contains" or "May contain". We read it together with the
      ingredients. If the photo doesn't show one, we say so and ask you to check the pack.
    Sugar
    Every added sugar on the list, under any of its names: sugar, liquid glucose, invert syrup, dextrose,
      candy sugar syrup and others. Ingredients are listed from most to least, so a sugar near the start is
      one of the main ingredients. When sugar appears under several names, each name sits lower on
      the list than their total would.
    Industrial ingredients
    Ingredients rarely used in home cooking, such as glucose syrup, maltodextrin, emulsifiers, modified
      starch or added flavouring. Researchers use them to spot "ultra-processed" food (the NOVA
      classification, group 4). It describes how a food is made. It is not advice about whether a food is
      right for you.
    Additive
    A substance added to do a job, such as a colour, a preservative or a raising agent. Most have an INS
      number, the international code printed on the pack, for example "Colour (129)". We check each one
      against FSSAI's list of additives permitted in India.
    Permitted in India
    FSSAI's additive rules (Appendix A of its food additive regulations) allow it in food, either freely,
      or up to a limit that depends on the kind of food. We record where in the rules it is allowed and the
      date we checked.
    Worth knowing
    An additive with research, or a regulator's opinion, that a shopper may want to read. It is permitted
      in India. Tap its tile to see what the research found, in plain words, and a link to the
      source. We write these only from the study or the regulator's own document.
    Not permitted in India
    An additive number that FSSAI's additive rules (Appendix A) give no use in any food. It is the most
      serious thing we say about a pack, so we show it only when the whole list was read clearly. We quote
      what the label prints and ask you to check the pack: if we misread the number, tap "Report a
      mistake". A pack with a barcode also goes to a person to check.
